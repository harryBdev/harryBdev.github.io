Instead of a prologue

Harry Brilakis · Written September 2024 · Published 26 November 2026 · Discuss on LinkedIn

In the early 1990s I found myself entering the NY job market, with a graduate engineering degree from a top-US University. Looking through the Help Wanted section of the NY Times, numerous ads covering at least several pages were looking for C/C++ programmers (remember, that was the pre-Java era), noting also the salaries offered. I consciously decided that would be my work area, as I was looking for a high-demand field. In my journey as a software engineer I met some great guys and gals. There was something pure in their endeavor with software: it was their natural language of thinking. Clear, precise and genuine. We were enjoying our work, although we knew that the (sometimes) amazing software structures that we were architecting would remain well hidden and would never be visible to public.

From the endless readings of that era, I recall two amazing books: effective C++ (by Scott Meyers) and Design Patterns (by Gamma et al). Alongside with the standard C/C++ book library (A Book on C, The C programming language, The C++ programming language, C++ primer), they were always on my work desk (and home desk as well).

When I returned back home to Greece (in the early 2000s), the software culture was immature. In addition, writing code was considered an entry level job (!), compensating with the corresponding salary range. Therefore, I chose a different career path, which led me to where I am now.

Since then, as my professional tasks were changing, I've read many other professional books, learned a couple of (national) languages and earned one more academic degree in business. My C/C++ language skills might have faded. But (I would like to believe that) the structured, deterministic thinking process is still present.… Perhaps now it is blended with more non-deterministic decision processing.

In the following posts, I will capture those elements of events that I consider worth recording, as I commonly return to those throughout my career.
